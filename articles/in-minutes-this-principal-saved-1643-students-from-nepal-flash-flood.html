In Minutes, This Principal Saved 1,643 Students From a Devastating Flash Flood in Nepal

August 27, 2026
“Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged, for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go.”
— Joshua 1:9

Imagine having only a few minutes to decide between waiting and saving over a thousand children. What would you do?

That was the situation facing Rajendra Dadabi, 47, principal of Tribhuvan Trishuli Secondary School in Nepal's Trishuli Valley, on August 26. It was around 9:20 a.m. local time. School activities were underway as usual — about 900 students were already on campus, while another 700 were still on their way, some on foot and some by bus.

Then a phone call changed everything. A friend of his from Betrawati, about five miles upstream, warned him that floodwaters were surging toward the school with tremendous force.

No Time to Wait

The situation forced Dadabi to make a decision immediately. Without waiting for things to get worse, he rang the school bell and led an evacuation, directing every student to leave the buildings and head for higher ground. He also managed to reach nearly all of the incoming school buses and told the drivers to turn around before they ever reached the campus.

Time was everything. In a short span of minutes, 1,643 students were evacuated and gathered in a place considered safer — beneath a bodhi tree on higher ground.

Not long after, something devastating happened. The three-story school building was swept away by a torrent carrying mud, rocks, and debris, disappearing entirely. A building that, minutes earlier, had been full of children's activity became part of the disaster zone.

Two Staff Members Lost, But Every Student Survived

The tragedy still left behind deep grief. Two members of the school staff were reported to have died in the disaster. But amid the destruction, there was one piece of news that became a ray of hope: not a single one of the 1,643 students lost their life.

The principal's swift decision mattered enormously. What Dadabi did may have lasted only a matter of minutes, but for thousands of children and their families, that decision was the difference between going home and becoming victims of the disaster.

When a Teacher Becomes a Rescuer

To some, a school principal might seem like simply someone responsible for running a school and its students. But in an emergency, that role can suddenly carry a much greater weight.

Dadabi didn't have much time to think things through. He knew one thing: the children had to be saved. And he chose to act.

This story reminds us that courage doesn't always look like it does in movies. There isn't always a hero's costume, a stage, or an award. Sometimes courage simply looks like someone staying calm while everyone else panics, then making the right decision at the most critical moment.

"Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged, for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go" (Joshua 1:9). On that day, Rajendra Dadabi wasn't just a school principal. He stood as the person who made sure 1,643 children still had the chance to go home and embrace their families.

One decision made in minutes. One school destroyed. Two lives lost. But 1,643 children saved. This is what it means to be a teacher when responsibility is truly tested by circumstance.
