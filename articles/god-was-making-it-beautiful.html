I Thought God Was Destroying My Life, But He Was Making It Beautiful

August 15, 2026
“No one serving as a soldier gets entangled in the concerns of civilian life; he seeks to please his commanding officer.”
— 2 Timothy 2:4

Have you ever asked God, "Why, after I surrendered my life to You, did even more problems come?" I have. I lost my father, my family struggled financially, my younger brother became trapped in drug addiction, and when I turned 30, I had never even been in a relationship. I thought God had forgotten me. I was wrong. He was writing a story far more beautiful than anything I could have imagined.

When My Life Seemed to Fall Apart

I was born and raised in a Christian family. When I was in junior high school, I decided to be baptized. But being baptized did not automatically mean that I was truly living out my faith.

It was not until I went to college that I began to truly experience a transformation in my relationship with God. I started serving Him and taking my faith seriously.

I thought that once I surrendered my life to God, everything would become better.

But it didn't.

When God Allowed Tragedy Into My Life

My father passed away. His death left a deep wound in my heart. It also caused our family's financial situation to become extremely difficult.

My mother eventually had to leave the country to work so she could provide for our family. But the problems didn't stop there. My younger brother became involved with drugs and eventually ended up in prison.

At that time, I was still in college. I felt overwhelmed. I watched my family struggle and couldn't understand why all of this was happening.

One day, through tears and prayer, I asked God: "Lord, haven't I already surrendered my life to You? Why are all these things happening?"

I didn't receive all the answers I wanted. But one thing remained certain: God was still there. And my mother remained strong. She worked tirelessly in a foreign country to support our family.

After graduating from college, I began working too, hoping to help my mother carry the family's burdens. I became so focused on work and responsibility that I didn't realize how quickly the years had passed.

Then I turned 30. And there was something else that made me feel increasingly pressured. I had never been in a relationship. People around me started asking: "When are you getting married?" At first, I tried not to think about it. But the more I heard it, the more anxious I became.

When I Had to Let Someone Go

One day, a friend introduced me to a man. We began getting to know each other and became friends. But deep down, I wasn't sure whether he was truly the person God had prepared for me.

One day, he asked me: "Would you rather follow what God says or what your heart says?" I told him that I would choose what God says. Not long after that, he slowly began to disappear from my life. Eventually, I saw him with another woman.

Even though we had only been friends, it still hurt. I cried. I prayed. And in the middle of my heartbreak, the Holy Spirit reminded me to obey God and surrender everything to Him.

That experience taught me something: Not everyone we want is meant to be part of our future. Sometimes God removes someone from our lives not because He wants to hurt us, but because He knows something we don't.

A Meeting I Never Planned

As time passed, I began living my life normally again. I stopped obsessing over when I would meet my future husband.

Then, in early March 2015, something unexpected happened. I was shopping at a supermarket when I suddenly met an old high school classmate whom I hadn't seen for about 12 years. We hadn't really known each other back then. He was one of the popular students at school, the student council president, and someone many girls admired.

I thought our meeting was simply a coincidence. But it wasn't.

A few months later, around July 2015, we began talking more often. We started getting to know each other. I discovered that he had experienced a transformation in his life and had genuinely come to know Christ. Our friendship grew closer.

Then, in October 2015, we decided to start dating. We didn't want our relationship to be based only on feelings. We sought guidance from spiritual mentors at church and learned how to build a relationship that honored God. We also committed ourselves to purity. For us, a relationship wasn't simply about having someone to love. It was about helping one another grow closer to God.

God Was Never Late

Now I understand. I once thought God was destroying my life. I didn't understand why my father had to die. I didn't understand why my family had to struggle financially. I didn't understand why my brother became trapped in drug addiction. I didn't understand why I had to wait so long for a life partner.

At times, I even asked: "God, do You really have a plan for my life?" But time revealed the answer. God had never lost control. Little by little, my family's financial situation began to recover. My brother was eventually freed from addiction. And God brought someone into my life through a simple meeting I never planned.

I learned that what looks like a delay to us may actually be God's preparation. What looks like a loss may become the beginning of something new. And what we call a tragedy may be something God is weaving into a testimony.

Don't Be Afraid of Your Future

Perhaps you are in the same place today. You've prayed, but problems keep coming. You've surrendered your life to God, but your family is still struggling. You've tried to remain faithful, but your future spouse still hasn't come. You see your friends getting married, building careers, and seemingly moving forward in life. Meanwhile, you're still asking: "When will it be my turn, Lord?"

Don't give up. God is never late. Don't measure God's love by your circumstances today. Don't assume that God has abandoned you simply because your journey looks different from everyone else's. There are things we can only understand after we've walked through them.

So keep believing. Keep serving. Keep your life surrendered to God. Because when we place our lives in His hands, nothing is ever wasted. Even the tears you shed in prayer can become part of a testimony that one day strengthens someone else.

As Scripture says: "No one serving as a soldier gets entangled in the concerns of civilian life; he seeks to please his commanding officer." — 2 Timothy 2:4

Maybe you don't understand God's plan today. That's okay. Keep trusting Him. Because the story God is writing in your life isn't finished yet. And perhaps the very thing you now see as a tragedy… is becoming the beginning of a beautiful testimony in God's hands.
