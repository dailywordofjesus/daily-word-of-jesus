10 Surprising Facts About the Bible You Probably Didn't Know

August 20, 2026
“Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path.”
— Psalm 119:105

The Bible is the most read, most translated, and most printed book in human history. But even lifelong believers are often surprised by how much they don't know about it. Here are ten facts that might change the way you see the book you read every day.

1. The Bible Wasn't Originally Written in English

The Old Testament was written primarily in Hebrew, with small portions in Aramaic. The New Testament was written in Koine Greek, the common language of the eastern Mediterranean at the time. Every English Bible you've ever read is a translation, and scholars continue to study the original manuscripts to make those translations as accurate as possible.

2. Chapter and Verse Numbers Were Added Centuries Later

The original texts of the Bible had no chapters or verses at all. Chapter divisions were added by Archbishop Stephen Langton around 1227, and verse numbers were added later by printer Robert Estienne in the 1550s. The system was created purely to help readers find and reference specific passages — it wasn't part of the original inspired text.

3. The Book of Esther Never Mentions God's Name

Esther is the only book in the entire Bible that does not explicitly mention God by name, not even once. Yet the entire story is built around His providence — a Jewish orphan becoming queen at exactly the right moment to save her people. It's a powerful reminder that God is often at work even when His presence isn't obvious.

4. It Took Over 1,500 Years to Write

The Bible wasn't written by one author in one sitting. It was composed by roughly 40 different writers across approximately 1,500 years, spanning kings, prophets, fishermen, physicians, and shepherds. Despite the vast difference in authors, eras, and writing styles, the Bible maintains a remarkably consistent central message from Genesis to Revelation.

5. Some Bibles Include Books That Others Don't

Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include a set of additional books known as the Apocrypha (or Deuterocanonical books), such as Tobit, Judith, and 1 and 2 Maccabees. Most Protestant Bibles do not include these books in their main canon. This difference dates back centuries and reflects different traditions about which ancient texts were considered divinely inspired scripture.

6. The Shortest Verse Is Just Two Words

In most English translations, John 11:35 reads simply: "Jesus wept." It's the shortest verse in the Bible, yet it carries enormous weight — showing that Jesus, though fully God, also fully shared in human grief and compassion at the death of His friend Lazarus.

7. The Bible Has Been Translated Into Over 3,000 Languages

According to Bible translation organizations, at least a portion of Scripture has been translated into more than 3,000 languages, and the complete Bible has been translated into hundreds of them. Translation work continues today, with organizations actively working to bring Scripture to language groups that still don't have it in their own tongue.

8. Jesus Is Quoted More in the Old Testament Than You'd Think

Throughout the Gospels, Jesus frequently quoted the Old Testament — from the Psalms, the Law, and the Prophets — treating those ancient texts as authoritative Scripture. When He was tempted in the wilderness, He responded to every temptation by quoting Deuteronomy. His deep familiarity with Scripture is a model for how believers are called to know God's Word.

9. It's Consistently the Best-Selling Book in the World

Estimates suggest more than 5 billion copies of the Bible have been printed and distributed throughout history, making it by far the best-selling book ever published. It continues to top annual bestseller lists worldwide, even though it has been in publication for centuries.

10. The Longest Chapter Is Entirely About God's Word

Psalm 119 is the longest chapter in the Bible, with 176 verses. Fittingly, its entire subject is a celebration of God's law, commands, and Word. It's structured as an acrostic poem in Hebrew, with each section beginning with a successive letter of the Hebrew alphabet — a literary structure designed to be memorized.

Why These Facts Matter

Knowing facts about the Bible is interesting, but it's not the point of Scripture. The Bible wasn't given to impress us with trivia — it was given to reveal who God is and how much He loves us. Psalm 119:105 says: "Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path."

Whether you've read the Bible cover to cover or are just getting started, there's always something new to discover — not just about the book itself, but about the God who inspired it.
