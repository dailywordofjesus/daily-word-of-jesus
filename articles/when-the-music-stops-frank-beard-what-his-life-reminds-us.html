When the Music Stops: What Frank Beard's Life Reminds Us About Living Well

August 21, 2026
“Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.”
— Psalm 90:12

Frank Beard, the longtime drummer for the rock band ZZ Top, has died at the age of 77. According to a statement from the band, he passed away in hospice care at his ranch in Texas, surrounded by family. For 56 years, his steady, disciplined beat was the backbone of one of rock's most recognizable sounds.

The One Who Stayed in the Background

Beard was, in many ways, the quiet one of the group. While his bandmates became famous for their long beards, sunglasses, and larger-than-life stage presence, Beard — ironically the only one without a beard — stayed seated behind the drum kit, keeping time, rarely seeking the spotlight for himself.

There's something worth pausing on in that. Not everyone is called to be the loudest voice in the room. Some of the most important work in any life — a family, a church, a workplace — is done by people who simply show up, keep the rhythm steady, and let others shine. Scripture speaks often of faithfulness in what looks like a small or hidden role: "Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much" (Luke 16:10).

A Life That Wasn't Without Struggle

Beard's life wasn't free of hardship. In interviews over the years, he was open about going through a difficult season with addiction earlier in his career, a time the band took a break so he could recover. He later returned, and continued playing with the same two bandmates for decades afterward — a rare thing in an industry known for lineup changes and broken relationships.

That kind of endurance — falling, recovering, and staying the course for over fifty years — is its own quiet testimony to the power of second chances. None of us know the full story of another person's private battles. What we can see is what they do with the years they're given after the hardest seasons pass.

The Reminder We Don't Like to Sit With

News like this has a way of stopping us for a moment, even if we never listened to a single ZZ Top song. A person who was present, working, showing up for over half a century is suddenly gone. It's a quiet nudge toward a truth we often avoid thinking about: our days are numbered, and none of us know exactly how many we have left.

The Bible doesn't shy away from this. Psalm 90:12 puts it plainly as a prayer: "Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom." It isn't meant to make us fearful. It's meant to make us present — to notice the people around us, to not put off the things that matter, to hold our time as something given to us rather than something we're owed.

What Kind of Beat Are We Keeping?

A drummer's job isn't to be noticed. It's to keep everyone else together — steady, dependable, present in every song whether anyone claps for it or not. There's a kind of faithfulness in that most of us are actually called to: showing up quietly, day after day, for the people who need us to be there.

As we hear this news, it's worth asking ourselves a simple question: if our life were measured not by how loud we were, but by how faithfully we kept showing up for the people around us — for God, for our families, for the small and unnoticed responsibilities of an ordinary day — what would that story look like so far?

Rest in peace, Frank Beard. And for the rest of us still keeping time: may we spend today a little more wisely than yesterday.
